The starting point

Lots of revenue data. No bandwidth to use it.

Stripe

Who actually pays. Subscriptions, invoices, failed and late payments. It's the most honest signal Hamina has, and it isn't used for forecasting.

HubSpot

Who is being sold to. Deals, stages, close dates. The forecast relies on stage percentages that nobody has checked against outcomes.

Ad platforms

What we spend to grow. Google, LinkedIn, Meta, YouTube, G2. Each reports its own conversions; none of it is tied to revenue.

Three systems with three versions of the truth. The questions the CRO, CFO and COO ask ("Will we hit plan?", "Which deals are real?", "Is marketing working?") need all three joined, modelled and kept fresh. That is a RevOps engineering job, not a spreadsheet.

What I built

One pipeline from raw data to decisions

Unify. Stripe ↔ HubSpot identity resolution (domain → contact email → fuzzy name). All 14 enterprise customers matched; 19 CRM companies already pay via self-serve.

Model. Renewal model, deal win/close-date model, marketing mix model. Everything is computed as of a date, so it can be backtested.

Deliver. Dashboard in Hamina's design system, AI weekly brief, and an MCP connector to ask Claude directly.

Forecast

FY2026 forecast €3.33M vs a €3.42M plan

  • 4,000 Monte Carlo runs combine renewals, new self-serve, enterprise renewals, open deals and not-yet-created pipeline.
  • FY2026 P10–P90 €3.16M–€3.55M: about a 29% chance of hitting plan.
  • FY2027 P50 €5.86M (+76%); ARR €6.1M by Dec 2027.
  • A range instead of one number, so finance can plan for P10 and sales can push for P90.
  • Today: ARR €3.17M (+66% YoY), NRR 84%, cash next 90 days €988k.

Marketing

Paid media drives 41% of self-serve bookings, with a lag

  • 6 platforms, 108 campaigns; paid ≈ 6–7% of revenue, in line with B2B SaaS benchmarks.
  • Lag matters: self-serve responds in 0–1 month; enterprise deals ~3 months later; revenue ~8 months.
  • Marketing mix model: €1.15 first-invoice bookings per €1 before renewals; carry-over half-life 1.6 months.
  • Honest limit: channel-level credit needs holdout tests; platform-reported CPLs are not revenue.

Model trust

Would it have been right? Backtested, not assumed.

  • The model is re-run as of 5 past dates using only data known then, then compared with what happened.
  • Average error 14% vs 28% for a naive run-rate; actuals inside the P10–P90 band 4 of 5 times.
  • The miss (mid-2025, −37%) is shown, not tuned away: two large wins the pipeline underrated. That's the next model improvement.
  • Component models: renewal AUC 0.63 (Stripe signals only), deal AUC 0.86.

Who it helps

One model, every function reads from it

  • Forecast with ranges for budget and board
  • 13-week cash view, overdue risk per payer
  • Plan vs P50 gap visible months early

Sales / CRO

  • Calibrated deal odds instead of stage %
  • Slipping and stalled deals flagged weekly
  • Paying self-serve accounts → expansion targets

Marketing

  • Spend tied to bookings and pipeline, with lag
  • Budget conversations in € of revenue, not CPL
  • Clear next step: holdout tests per channel

RevOps / GTM

  • Stripe ↔ HubSpot identity resolved
  • Data-quality signals (missing domains, pushes)
  • Write-back of scores into HubSpot

Customer success

  • Renewal risk list with reasons, 180 days ahead
  • Late-payment and failed-card early warnings
  • Prioritise by ARR at risk

COO / company ops

  • One shared number across teams
  • Hiring and spend decisions from scenarios
  • Less manual reporting: brief and Claude answer
